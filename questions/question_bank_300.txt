Which statement best describes anxiety?
A. A specific, identifiable fear of a known object
B. A vague feeling of apprehension or fear whose source is not always known or recognized
C. A fixed false belief that persists despite evidence
D. A depressed mood lasting at least two years
Answer: B
Why: Anxiety is a vague apprehension or fear; the source is not always known, which adds to the distress.

Which statement best describes anxiety disorders?
A. Brief responses that never affect functioning
B. Conditions caused only by substance use
C. Disorders defined by loss of contact with reality
D. A group of conditions sharing excessive anxiety with behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and physiologic responses
Answer: D
Why: Excessive anxiety with ensuing behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and physiologic responses is the key shared feature.

D. Covertly aggressive
Answer: C
Why: Clients are withdrawn and engage in odd behavior.

Cluster B personality disorders are described as:
A. Odd and eccentric
B. Erratic, dramatic, and emotional
C. Anxious and fearful
D. Passive
Answer: B
Why: Cluster B clients are attention seekers.

Cluster C personality disorders are described as:
A. Odd
B. Dramatic
C. Erratic
D. Anxious and fearful, seeking to avoid or minimize anxiety
Answer: D
Why: Cluster C includes avoidant, dependent, and obsessive-compulsive.

Which disorders belong to Cluster A?
A. Paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal
B. Antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic
C. Avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive
D. Passive-aggressive
Answer: A
Why: These are the odd and eccentric disorders.

Which personality disorder is listed as "not specified" and described as covertly aggressive?
A. Narcissistic
B. Borderline
C. Passive-aggressive
D. Dependent
Answer: C
Why: Passive-aggressive PD is categorized outside the three clusters.

The prevalence rate of personality disorders given in the lecture is:
A. 1.3%
B. 13.4%
C. 50%
D. 0.2%
Answer: B
Why: The most common are avoidant, schizoid, and paranoid.

The etiology of personality disorders is:
A. A single clear cause

C. Infection
D. Largely unknown, with multiple causes including genetic, environmental factors, and temperament
Answer: D
Why: There is no clear-cut single cause.

Schizoid personality disorder is characterized by:
A. A pervasive pattern of detachment from social relationships and restricted range of emotional expression
B. Magical thinking
C. Mistrust
D. Need for attention
Answer: A
Why: These clients avoid treatment as much as they avoid relationships.

Which assessment findings apply to schizoid PD?
A. Seductive behavior
B. Grandiosity
C. Aloofness, interest in solitary activities, indifference to praise and criticism, lack of future goals
D. Impulsivity
Answer: C
Why: They may succeed in jobs like computers with little contact with others.

Which findings are characteristic of schizotypal PD?
A. Charm
B. Magical thinking, odd thinking and ideas of reference, bizarre speech, flat affect, anxiety around others
C. Dramatics
D. Perfectionism
Answer: B
Why: Schizotypal PD includes cognitive or perceptual distortions and eccentric behavior.

Nursing management for schizotypal PD includes:
A. Confrontation
B. Isolation
C. Limit setting only
D. Encouraging daily hygiene and grooming routines, role playing interactions, and social skills training
Answer: D
Why: The goal is improved self-care and social functioning in the community.

Paranoid PD is characterized by:
A. Pervasive mistrust and suspiciousness of others, interpreting others' actions as potentially harmful
B. Clinging behavior
C. Perfectionism
D. Seductiveness
Answer: A
Why: Transient psychotic symptoms may occur during stress.

Which defense mechanism is used by clients with paranoid PD to blame others?
A. Denial
B. Repression
C. Projection
D. Sublimation
Answer: C
Why: Projection is the lecture's stated defense mechanism.

Which nursing approach is best for a client with paranoid PD?
A. Joking and teasing
B. A serious, straightforward approach, teaching the client to validate ideas before acting, and involving him in care planning
C. Whispering with other staff
D. Vague explanations
Answer: B
Why: Honesty and involvement build trust.

Antisocial PD is characterized by:
A. Clinging
B. Perfectionism
C. Detachment
D. A pervasive pattern of disregard for and violation of the rights of others, with deceit and manipulation
Answer: D
Why: Onset is in childhood or adolescence but diagnosis is made at 18.

Which is characteristic of clients with antisocial PD?
A. Skill at deceiving others, no shame or guilt, inability to empathize, poor judgment, easily bored
B. Intense guilt
C. Hallucinations
D. Low IQ
Answer: A
Why: They are oriented, with average or above-average IQs.

Which nursing intervention is most important for antisocial PD?
A. Giving gifts
B. Allowing rule breaking
C. Limit setting, and teaching problem-solving and anger/frustration management
D. Ignoring the behavior
Answer: C
Why: Limits decrease manipulative behavior.

Borderline PD is characterized by:
A. Detachment
B. A pervasive pattern of unstable interpersonal relationships, self-image, and affect, with marked impulsivity
C. Grandiosity
D. Perfectionism
Answer: B
Why: The pervasive mood is dysphoric.

"Splitting" in borderline PD refers to:
A. Physical separation
B. Dissociation
C. Memory loss
D. Extreme, all-or-nothing thinking about self and others
Answer: D
Why: Thinking is often extreme.

Which fear is characteristic of borderline PD?
A. Extreme fear of abandonment, with difficulty believing a relationship exists once the person is away
B. Fear of heights
C. Fear of crowds
D. Fear of authority
Answer: A
Why: They also have self-destructive behavior and cannot tolerate anxiety.

Which nursing interventions apply to borderline PD?
A. Encourage dependence
B. Ignore self-harm
C. Promote safety, help cope with and control emotions, use cognitive restructuring, teach socialization skills
D. Avoid limits
Answer: C
Why: These are the listed interventions.

Histrionic PD is characterized by:
A. Detachment
B. Excessive emotionality and attention seeking
C. Suspiciousness
D. Perfectionism
Answer: B
Why: These clients are lively, dramatic, and enjoy being the center of attention.

Which nursing interventions are used for histrionic PD?
A. Giving praise for every complaint
B. Ignoring the client
C. Joining the drama
D. Teaching social skills and providing factual feedback about behavior
Answer: D
Why: Factual feedback replaces theatrical responses.

Narcissistic PD is characterized by:
A. Grandiosity, need for admiration, and lack of empathy
B. Clinging
C. Mistrust
D. Social inhibition
Answer: A
Why: They overestimate abilities and underestimate others' contributions.

A client with narcissistic PD becomes angry when he does not receive special treatment. Which nursing approach is best?
A. Argue
B. Give special treatment
C. A matter-of-fact (straightforward and unemotional) approach
D. Ignore him
Answer: C
Why: The nurse gains cooperation and teaches self-care skills.

Avoidant PD is characterized by:
A. Grandiosity
B. Social discomfort and reticence, low self-esteem, and hypersensitivity to negative evaluation
C. Mistrust
D. Impulsivity
Answer: B
Why: These clients fear rejection and criticism and lack a support system.

Nursing interventions for avoidant PD include:
A. Confrontation
B. Isolation
C. Limit setting
D. Support and reassurance, cognitive restructuring, and promotion of self-esteem
Answer: D
Why: These address inadequacy and fear of criticism.

Dependent PD is characterized by:
A. A pervasive and excessive need to be taken care of, leading to submissive, clinging behavior and fear of separation
B. Detachment
C. Manipulation
D. Superiority
Answer: A
Why: They have difficulty making decisions and cannot tolerate being alone.

Nursing interventions for dependent PD include:
A. Making decisions for the client
B. Encouraging reliance
C. Fostering self-reliance and autonomy, and teaching problem-solving and decision-making
D. Discharging early
Answer: C
Why: Autonomy replaces dependence.

Obsessive-compulsive PD is characterized by:
A. Impulsivity
B. Preoccupation with perfectionism, mental and interpersonal control, and orderliness at the expense of flexibility
C. Dramatics
D. Detachment
Answer: B
Why: These clients are devoted to work, lack leisure, are miserly, and may hoard worthless objects.

Nursing interventions for obsessive-compulsive PD include:
A. Encouraging rigid rules
B. Giving unlimited time
C. Doing tasks for the client
D. Encouraging negotiation, helping make timely decisions and complete work, cognitive restructuring
Answer: D
Why: These counter inflexibility and indecision.

Passive-aggressive PD is characterized by:
A. Passive resistance to demands for adequate social and occupational performance, with a negative attitude
B. Grandiosity
C. Clinging
D. Detachment
Answer: A
Why: Clients complain of being misunderstood and unappreciated.

How do passive-aggressive clients express resistance?
A. Open aggression
B. Withdrawal
C. Procrastination, forgetfulness, stubbornness, and intentional inefficiency
D. Seduction
Answer: C
Why: This is especially true in response to tasks assigned by authority figures.

Which is a recommended way to handle manipulative behavior?
A. Accept small gifts
B. Set clear, realistic limits and confront the client about manipulative behavior
C. Keep the client's behavior secret
D. Give extra favors
Answer: B
Why: The nurse accepts no gift or flattery and forms a therapeutic relationship.

How should the nurse communicate with other staff about a manipulative client?
A. Avoid communication
B. Share only verbal reports
C. Let each nurse decide
D. Clearly and consistently communicate care plans and client behaviors to other nurses
Answer: D
Why: Consistency prevents splitting.

Which action is recommended when a client's attention-seeking behavior occurs in a group?
A. Remove the client from the group situation
B. Praise the behavior
C. Give a gift
D. Ignore the group
Answer: A
Why: Realistic praise is given for positive behavior in social situations.

Which interventions are part of general nursing management for personality disorders?
A. Allowing manipulation
B. Using inconsistent responses
C. Providing a written contract, encouraging journaling, limit setting, safety, consistency, and a therapeutic relationship
D. Isolating the client
Answer: C
Why: Consistency, limits, and discussing feelings rather than acting them out are central.
